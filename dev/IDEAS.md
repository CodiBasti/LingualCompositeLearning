## 💡 Idea Repository

### 1. Phonetic & Morphological Design of Concepts (Bouba/Kiki Symbolism)
- **Concept:** Should synthetic operators incorporate sound symbolism (the Bouba/Kiki effect)?
  - Concepts with soft continuants/liquids (e.g. `weli`, `somo`, `fufu`) intuitively suggest gentle compliance and spatial cushioning.
  - Concepts with unvoiced plosives (e.g. `kuku`, `gugi`) signal rigid stop boundaries, hard constraints, or emergency overrides.
- **Cognitive Utility:** Does sound symbolism strengthen human retention and intuitive association of safety invariants during human-robot co-presence?

### 2. Multimodal & Gestural Symbols as Concept Representations
- **Concept:** Beyond spoken/written tokens, multimodal glyphs or robot kinesthetic gestures (e.g. specific LED pulse sequences, AR/HUD icons on Vision Pro / Quest) could signify the active concept operator.
- **Cognitive Utility:** A universal icon for `fufu` on a control station or wearable display directly aligns the human mental model with the robot's latent execution manifold.

### 3. Dynamic Concept Negotiation (Interactive Calibration)
- **Concept:** When a human introduces a novel operational concept (*"From now on, handle this zuzu"*), the embodied AI interactively clarifies: *"Does zuzu in this workspace imply halting at >10% sensor ambiguity, or requesting vocal authorization?"*.
- **Cognitive Utility:** Incremental Bayesian calibration of shared mutual semantics between a specific user and their autonomous assistive robot.

### 4. Concept Composition as an Algebraic Structure
- **Concept:** Defining a formal concept algebra:
  - $A \oplus B$: Union and intersection of behavioral modifiers.
  - Conflict resolution: When $B_1$ (operational haste) and $B_2$ (clearance caution) collide, which invariants dominate? (Formally established in the Safety Dominance Principle: $\mathcal{D}_{\text{risk}} \succ \mathcal{D}_{\text{time}}$).

### 5. Multilingual Cross-Cultural Transcendence
- **Concept:** How does a synthetic CVCV vocabulary interact with native speakers of non-Indo-European languages (e.g. Mandarin, Arabic, Japanese)? Does a synthetic, language-neutral vocabulary overcome mother-tongue semantic biases more effectively than translated natural language terminology?
