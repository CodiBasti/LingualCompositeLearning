# Literature Note: Lupyan et al. (2007) - Language Is Not Just for Talking

- **Citation:** Lupyan, G., Rakison, D. H. & McClelland, J. L. (2007): *Language Is Not Just for Talking: Redundant Labels Facilitate Learning of Novel Categories*. Psychological Science.
- **Source:** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18031415/
- **Relevance for Lingual Composite Learning:** High (Grounding the human cognitive intention formation component / H1).
- **Category:** Psycholinguistics & Cognitive Psychology.

---

## 1. Core Contribution & Thesis
The presence of explicit linguistic labels facilitates human acquisition and retention of novel visual-conceptual categories, even when the label is functionally redundant and provides no additional category information. Language functions not merely as an external transmission tool, but as a cognitive feedback catalyst shaping internal mental representations (the Label-Feedback Hypothesis).

## 2. Methodology & Experiments
Empirical laboratory study with human subjects: participants learned synthetic categories ("aliens") either accompanied by novel verbal labels or without linguistic tagging. The labeled cohort mastered category boundaries significantly faster and generalized far more robustly to edge cases.

## 3. Direct Relevance to Our Research
- Theoretical grounding for Hypothesis H1: when human operators acquire the label `fufu`, they recall the clustered safety invariants (prudence, perimeter observation, speed derating) as a coherent mental unit more reliably.

## 4. Limitations & Research Gap
- Experiments were confined to static visual stimuli classification, rather than sequential dynamic planning, human-AI instruction, or safety-critical robot actuation.
